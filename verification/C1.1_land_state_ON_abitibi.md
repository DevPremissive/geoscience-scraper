**Two findings from the exercise, both about the parks layer:**

1. **The stack catches an encumbrance MLAS does not expose.** Provincial parks
   were correctly excluded even though that layer could not be selected in the
   viewer. A human working only in MLAS would not readily see why that ground is
   unavailable.

2. **…but the source is a geology publication, not a parks registry.** Those
   polygons come from `MRD126-REV1` (1:250 000 Bedrock Geology of Ontario),
   where park outlines are basemap furniture. They are a snapshot from whenever
   that map was compiled, and nothing about it tracks park regulation. Parks get
   created, expanded and amended; this layer will not follow. Both error
   directions are live — a park regulated since MRD126 would leave its ground
   reading `open`.

   This makes registering LIO's authoritative protected-areas layer do three
   jobs at once: add the missing conservation reserves (~15,000 km²), put park
   boundaries on a maintained source, and stop a legal encumbrance depending on
   a geology publication. Recommended before any Ontario dossier ships.
